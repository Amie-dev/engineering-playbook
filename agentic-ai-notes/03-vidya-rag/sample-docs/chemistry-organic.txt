CHAPTER: Organic Chemistry - Hydrocarbons

TOPIC: Classification of Hydrocarbons
Hydrocarbons are compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms.

Types:
1. Saturated hydrocarbons (Alkanes): Only single bonds between carbon atoms. General formula: CnH2n+2. Example: Methane (CH4), Ethane (C2H6).
2. Unsaturated hydrocarbons:
   a. Alkenes: Contain at least one carbon-carbon double bond. General formula: CnH2n. Example: Ethene (C2H4).
   b. Alkynes: Contain at least one carbon-carbon triple bond. General formula: CnH2n-2. Example: Ethyne (C2H2).
3. Aromatic hydrocarbons: Contain a benzene ring. Example: Benzene (C6H6), Toluene.

TOPIC: IUPAC Nomenclature
Steps for naming organic compounds:
1. Find the longest continuous carbon chain (parent chain).
2. Number the carbons starting from the end nearest to the first branch.
3. Name each substituent with its position number.
4. Use prefixes: meth (1C), eth (2C), prop (3C), but (4C), pent (5C), hex (6C).
5. Use suffixes: -ane (single bond), -ene (double bond), -yne (triple bond).

Example: CH3-CH(CH3)-CH2-CH3 is 2-methylbutane, not 3-methylbutane. Always use the lowest locant.

JEE Important: For compounds with both double and triple bonds, the numbering should give the lowest set of locants. The suffix becomes -en-yne.

TOPIC: Isomerism
Isomers are compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural arrangements.

Types of structural isomerism:
1. Chain isomerism: Different carbon chain arrangements. Butane and isobutane (2-methylpropane) are chain isomers.
2. Position isomerism: Same functional group at different positions. 1-propanol and 2-propanol.
3. Functional group isomerism: Different functional groups. Ethanol (C2H5OH) and dimethyl ether (CH3OCH3).

TOPIC: Chemical Reactions of Alkanes
1. Combustion: CH4 + 2O2 -> CO2 + 2H2O + energy. Complete combustion gives CO2 and water.
2. Substitution: In presence of UV light, hydrogen atoms are replaced by halogens.
   CH4 + Cl2 -> CH3Cl + HCl (in sunlight)
   This follows a free radical mechanism: initiation, propagation, termination.
3. Pyrolysis (Cracking): Breaking larger alkanes into smaller ones at high temperature.

TOPIC: Chemical Reactions of Alkenes
1. Addition reactions (characteristic of unsaturated compounds):
   a. Hydrogenation: CH2=CH2 + H2 -> CH3-CH3 (using Ni catalyst)
   b. Halogenation: CH2=CH2 + Br2 -> CH2Br-CH2Br (decolorizes bromine water - test for unsaturation)
   c. Hydrohalogenation: CH2=CH2 + HBr -> CH3-CH2Br

2. Markovnikov's Rule: When HX adds to an unsaturated bond, H goes to the carbon with more hydrogens.
   CH3-CH=CH2 + HBr -> CH3-CHBr-CH3 (not CH3-CH2-CH2Br)

   Anti-Markovnikov addition (Peroxide effect): In presence of peroxide, Br goes to the carbon with more hydrogens.

NEET Tip: Markovnikov's rule is frequently tested. Remember - "the rich get richer" - hydrogen goes to the carbon that already has more hydrogens.

TOPIC: Chemical Reactions of Alkynes
1. Alkynes undergo addition reactions similar to alkenes but can add two molecules.
2. Acidic nature of terminal alkynes: Terminal alkynes (with H on triple-bond carbon) react with sodium metal or ammoniacal silver nitrate.
   HC triple bond CH + Na -> HC triple bond CNa + 1/2 H2
   This is because the sp hybridized carbon is more electronegative.

TOPIC: Benzene and Aromaticity
Benzene has a planar hexagonal structure with all C-C bonds of equal length (1.39 Angstrom).
Huckel's rule: A compound is aromatic if it has (4n+2) pi electrons in a planar cyclic arrangement.
Benzene has 6 pi electrons (n=1), making it aromatic.

Reactions of benzene are mainly electrophilic substitution (not addition, to preserve aromaticity):
1. Nitration: C6H6 + HNO3 -> C6H5NO2 + H2O (using H2SO4 catalyst)
2. Halogenation: C6H6 + Cl2 -> C6H5Cl + HCl (using AlCl3 catalyst)
3. Friedel-Crafts alkylation: C6H6 + CH3Cl -> C6H5CH3 + HCl (using AlCl3)
